## Think before you ask

Something every programmer and developer can agree on is running into problems in their code. Whether this be an unexpected output or a type of error, we all have problems we need to solve. Now your first instinct might be to ask someone who might be more experienced. Well Eric Raymond, a well known American software developer, actually advices against this.

Before you even ask, he strongly recommends to do as much research and thinking as you can. This includes looking on google, web forums, FAQs, etc. And if you still can't find your answer, it allows you to ask a well thought out question and shows others you have done your own research and are willing and capable of learning.

## Now what is a smart question?

Raymond stresses the importance of meaningful, specific, clear, and descriptive questions. The trick to getting answers starts with spelling and grammar. Don't write like you're talking to a friend. Spell things correctly, add punctuation and capitalize when appropriate. Even a 'please' and 'thank you' can improve your chances of a smart answer.

Next the actual problem. Describe any relevant information about your problem. The symptoms of the problem, the environment it occurs, and your overall goal. This is where the previous research and steps you took to understand and remedy the problem comes into play.
